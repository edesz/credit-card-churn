Project Scoping

Author

edesz

Last Updated

May 7, 2026

About

This step of the analysis performs project scoping using the full random sample of ~10,000 credit card customers’ data. We will use the analysis performed here to quantify the impact of the problem of credit card customer churn facing the client with two metrics for measuring churn.

We will load and combine the pre-processed data splits in order to quantify the impact of the problem across all customers.

First, we will calculate the churn (or churn rate) for comparison to the industry average across the financial sector. If the bank’s observed churn is higher than that of its peers across the industry then it is worth exploring a ML approach to classify existing at-risk customers and target them to try to get them to revert their decision to cancel their services at the bank.

We will then estimate the lost Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) per churned customer. Similar to churn, if this impact is higher than the industry average then this is another reason for the client to intervene.

If both of these metrics are not higher than the industry average then that reduces the importance of dedicating funding to target the at-risk customers.

columns = [

multiplier = (1 - discount**tenure_years) / (1 - discount)

Load and combine a subset of columns from the pre-processed data across all three data splits

dfs = []
response = s3_client.list_objects_v2(Bucket=bucket_name, Prefix=prefix)

Calculate Customer Lifetime Value

We will first estimate the three sources of credit-card revenue, then combine them into the annual revenue and then use it to estimate the customer lifetime value (CLV) for each customer

df = df.assign(
    interchange_rev=lambda df: df["total_trans_amt"] * interchange_rate,
    interest_rev=lambda df: df["total_revolv_bal"] * apr,
    fee_rev=lambda df: (
        df["card_category"].map(card_fees).astype("int16[pyarrow]")
    ),
    annual_rev=lambda df: (
        df["interchange_rev"] + df["interest_rev"] + df["fee_rev"]
    ),
    clv=lambda df: df["annual_rev"] * multiplier,
    intervention_cost=intervention_cost,
)

Estimate Impact of Churn

Next, we estimate the two business metrics to quantify the impact of churn at the client’s bank.

TipTerms Used to Estimate CLV

The first metric is the churn (or churn rate), which is taken directly from the class imbalance of the is_churned column.

The second metric is the CLV of existing customers, which was estimated above

Fraction of Churned Customers (Churn Rate) vs Industry Average

The churn rate at the bank is calculated below

df_attrition_rate = (
    df["is_churned"]
    .value_counts(normalize=True)
    .mul(100)
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"is_churned": "attrition"})
    .assign(
        attrition=lambda df: df["attrition"].map(
            {False: "No Churn", True: "Churn"}
        )
    )
    .query("attrition == 'Churn'")
)

The bank’s credit card churn rate is now combined with that of the financial sector overall and the high credit card churn level

Observations

  1. Credit card churn is observed in 16% of customers. This is taken as the churn rate and is
    • just below the industry average churn rate for the financial sector of 19%
    • below the standard range for high credit card churn of 25-30%
    Although data is not provided by the client, the client observed churn to be increasing at the bank. Since the observed churn is just below the industry average, it would be beneficial to target at-risk customers now to prevent them from churning in the future.

Non-Churned CLV vs Industry Average

The total number of customers and total Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) are shown below for churned and existing customers

df_attrition_stats = (
    df
    .groupby('is_churned')
    .agg({'clv': ['sum', 'count']})
    .set_axis(['clv_total', 'num_customers'], axis=1)
    .reset_index()
    .assign(
        clv_fraction=lambda df: (
            (df['clv_total']/df['clv_total'].sum()).mul(100)
        ),
        clv_per_customer=lambda df: df['clv_total']/df['num_customers'],
    )
)

We can estimate the financial cost of churn by calculating the lost CLV per churned customer and add the replacement cost (CAC). The average CAC for a credit card customer is approximately 200 dollars. The cost of churn is estimated below

df_attr_stats_impact = (
    df_attrition_stats.reset_index(drop=True)
    .assign(
        impact_per_customer=lambda df: df["clv_per_customer"]
        + replacement_cost,
        impact_total=lambda df: (
            df["clv_total"] + df["num_customers"].mul(replacement_cost)
        ),
    )
    .query("is_churned == True")
)

Approximately 10% of customer lifetime value is lost due to the 16% churn rate over the past 12 months in the random sample of customer data. The impact to the client (i.e. to the bank’s credit card division) is a loss of approximately 508 dollars of customer lifetime value (per customer). When the cost to acquire a new customer is taken into account this adds up to a loss of 708 dollars per customer, or 1,151,828 dollars overall, due to churn.

Conclusion

One of the churn metrics calculated here (churn impact) indicate the bank is already worse off than its peers. The other metric (churn rate) is just three percentage points below the industry average and is increasing. So, it is worth assigning funding now to identify and target customers at risk of canceling their credit card services so churn at the bank does not fall behind its competitors in the near future.
